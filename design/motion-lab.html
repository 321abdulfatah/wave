WAVE — Motion Lab

your door, answered without a word

The resident cannot hear. Every animation below is doing the job a sound does in an ordinary doorbell, and each one is labelled with the sound it replaces. Nothing here is decorative: if an animation has no audio counterpart and no informational job, it was cut. Full spec and research: docs/motion.md.

This page deliberately breaks the product’s own rule that only one loop may run at a time (§9), so that every animation can be inspected at once. The product never does this.
T0 · DOOR LIVE

The Ring

Someone is at the door right now. Must be caught in peripheral vision from across a room, must never approach seizure territory, must sustain for the whole live event without becoming punishing. Three expanding rings leave the door position; the display edge blooms; the field swells. Expansion is used instead of frequency because looming motion captures attention regardless of where the eye is pointed — and it is safe at 0.83 Hz, which flicker is not.

T2 · CONVERSATION

Utterance

The Chime is speaking to the visitor and the resident cannot hear it. The caption carries what is said; the bars carry that it is being said, now, at this pace — which matters because the visitor is reacting in real time. The flat runs are phrase gaps: the silences are the information. Bars are driven by TTS word-boundary events, not by a random equalizer.

T2 · CONVERSATION

Confidence Build

The camera is building confidence toward recognising a hand. This is the visitor’s only cue that they have been understood, and they are standing outside in the cold. The arc is bound to the recogniser’s confidence value and nothing else — watch it retreat at ~1.4 s when confidence drops. A ring that fills smoothly and then rejects is worse than no feedback: it teaches the visitor that the feedback is theatre.

T2 · RESOLUTION

Seal — accepted

The gesture was understood. Radial, centred, one outward release — the latch clunking home, then a single ripple of confirmation. Distinguished from rejection by axis and duration, not by colour, because peripheral colour discrimination is poor and some Deaf users are also colour-blind.

T2 · RESOLUTION

Refuse — rejected

The gesture was not understood. Lateral, decaying, never completes — the head-shake made physical. The decay is what makes it read as friction rather than a rendering fault, and the ring is left visibly broken so the state survives the animation ending.

T3 · AMBIENT

Slip-in

      T3 · AMBIENT

      Resolve

      An indeterminate process ends. For a hearing user this was the moment a sound stopped — and silence is invisible. So the loop is never allowed to simply vanish: it snaps into a determinate shape, and the label changes with it. A loop that just disappears cannot be told apart from a crash.

      T1 · SYSTEM BLIND

      Signal Lost / Restored

      The most dangerous state in the product: if the camera is offline the resident is not being told about their door and does not know it. So offline is not a quiet grey — it is the one condition besides the doorbell allowed to persist. Colour drains over 560 ms (a loss gesture), a static hatch lands, and the dot breathes at 0.42 Hz. Recovery gets exactly one pulse.

      Attention hierarchy

      Three things want attention at once: the door is live, the camera dropped earlier, and a delivery was just logged. Two sounds mix and you still hear both — two competing loops and you read neither. Toggle the rule off to see what the unmanaged version costs.

      Rule 1
      One looping animation at a time, globally
      Rule 2
      One-shots stagger by ≥ 120 ms — closer than ~100 ms and two onsets fuse into one
      Rule 3
      Nothing outranks T0; arrivals during a live door event are silent
      Rule 4
      Escalation may interrupt; de-escalation always plays its ≥ 240 ms resolve
      Rule 5
      Peripheral budget: one element, T0 or T1 only

      With the rule off, all three lanes loop at once at three different periods. Nothing reads as urgent because everything does.

      Every animation on this page answers one question: what would this have been if the user could hear? The doorbell is the chime. Utterance is the voice. Confidence Build is “mm-hm”. Seal is the latch. Refuse is “sorry, what?”. Resolve is the moment the sound stopped. Signal Lost is dead air. Anything with no audio counterpart — hover bloom, staggered reveals, parallax, skeleton shimmer, a spinner during the gesture read — was cut.

      Constraints that set the numbers: WCAG 2.3.1/2.3.2 (three flashes per second), the clinical photoparoxysmal floor of 3 Hz with a peak near 16 Hz, WCAG 2.2.2 (pause/stop/hide), NFPA 72 visible notification appliances at 1–2 Hz, and the finding that looming expansion captures attention regardless of where the eye is pointed. Full citations in docs/motion.md.
