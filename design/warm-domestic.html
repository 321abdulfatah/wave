The five signs your door understands

Gesture vocabulary

  • Thumbs up

    “Yes”

    Agreement, or go ahead.

  • Open palm

    “Wait”

    Hold on — I need a moment.

  • Wave

    “Hello, I am a person”

    Opens a conversation, not a delivery.

  • Point down

    “Leaving it here”

    Placed on the step or in the porch.

  • Closed fist

    “No”

    Declines, or answers no.

Loud without sound

The alert never turns red. It warms: ochre light gathers, three rings open out from the chime, and the strip along the top edge drifts like daylight under a door. Motion and heat do the work an alarm tone would.

A house, not a console

Cream paper, clay ink, a sage tick for things that went well. Rounded forms, ruled lines, a wax-seal wordmark. Nothing here is tactical, because nothing at your own front door should be.

The transcript is the product

Set like a letter, in Fraunces, at reading size — not a log at 12px. The door speaks in italic, the visitor answers in upright type with the gesture beside it, so the two voices never blur.
